    What the "Colours" of Noise Mean

    All three contain every audible frequency at once, the difference is the balance:

    White Noise

    Equal energy at every frequency, so the high frequencies are prominent. It sounds bright and hissy, think untuned radio static, a fan, or an air conditioner. Its broad spectrum can mask many environmental sounds, although effectiveness depends on the sound being masked and the listening level.

    Pink Noise

    Energy drops as frequency rises, so it is perceived as softer than white noise and the highs are less prominent. It sounds like steady rain, wind through trees, or a waterfall heard from a distance. Some listeners prefer it to white noise, but research does not establish it as better for a full night's sleep.

    Brown Noise

    Energy drops off even faster toward the highs, so it is deep and rumbly with little hiss, like heavy rainfall, a low waterfall, or ocean surf. Its bass-heavy character may appeal to people who find white noise too sharp, but evidence for sleep or focus benefits is limited.

    Which Noise Is Best for What?

    For Sleep

    There is no single winner. Choose by preference and by the background sound you want to mask. White or pink noise may cover higher-frequency disruption; brown noise has less hiss. Systematic reviews find mixed, low-certainty evidence for continuous noise as a sleep aid, so treat it as an optional environmental tool, not a treatment for insomnia.

    For Focus and Studying

    Brown noise is a popular preference for focus because its low rumble may mask background chatter without much hiss. Research has not established brown noise as a treatment for ADHD or a reliable way to improve concentration.

    For Blocking Out Noise

    White noise spans the audible range and may mask voices or clatter, but the result depends on the competing sound and volume. Do not raise it to unsafe levels to overpower a loud environment.

    For Babies

    Some families use white noise for infants, but it is optional and evidence of sleep benefit is limited. Keep the volume low, place the speaker as far from the sleep space as practical, limit duration, and keep devices and cords out of the crib.     Frequently Asked Questions

    Is brown noise or white noise better for sleep?

    Research does not identify a clear winner. White noise spans more high frequencies, while brown noise has a deeper sound with less hiss. If you try either, keep the volume low and judge it by comfort rather than expecting a guaranteed sleep benefit.

    What is the difference between white, pink, and brown noise?

    All three contain every frequency, but the balance differs. White is bright and hissy (equal energy everywhere), pink is softer and balanced (like steady rain), and brown is deep and rumbly with little hiss (like heavy rain or surf).

    Which noise is best for focus or studying?

    No colour has been established as best. Brown noise is a popular preference because it has less high-frequency hiss, but it is not a proven treatment for attention problems. Use whichever low-volume sound is least distracting.
