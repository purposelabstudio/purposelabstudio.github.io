Blood Pressure Category Checker

Enter a reading to see its AHA category. Reference only, the check runs entirely in your browser, nothing is stored or sent.

The American Heart Association classifies blood pressure as Normal (below 120/80 mmHg), Elevated (120–129 and below 80), Stage 1 Hypertension (130–139 or 80–89), Stage 2 Hypertension (140+ or 90+), and Hypertensive Crisis (above 180/120). Based on American Heart Association (AHA) cut-offs. Educational reference only, not a diagnosis. Always confirm with your doctor.

How this works

This page does one mechanical job: it compares two numbers against a published set of cut-offs and reports which band they land in. It does not interpret the result, and it does not know anything about you. The thresholds it applies are written out in full below, and an automated test checks them on every change to this site, so the published cut-offs and the working ones cannot drift apart.

Using the checker

  1. Enter the systolic number, the higher figure on your monitor.
  2. Enter the diastolic number, the lower figure.
  3. Select Check category to see which band the reading falls into.

The cut-offs it applies

All figures are in millimetres of mercury (mmHg), and they are the American Heart Association's published boundaries:

  • Hypertensive Crisis: systolic 180 or above, or diastolic 120 or above.
  • High Blood Pressure (Stage 2): systolic 140 or above, or diastolic 90 or above.
  • High Blood Pressure (Stage 1): systolic 130 or above, or diastolic 80 or above.
  • Elevated: systolic 120 to 129, with diastolic below 80.
  • Normal: anything below those lines.

Why the order of the checks matters

The bands are tested from the most severe downwards, and the first one that matches is the one reported. That single rule is what produces the behaviour people find surprising: a reading of 122/82 comes back as Stage 1, because the lower number has already crossed the Stage 1 line even though the upper number on its own would read as Elevated. Whichever of the two figures sits in the worse band decides the result.

The comparison runs entirely in your browser. Readings are not stored, not sent anywhere, and not kept once the tab is closed. As the note above says, this is a reference for where a reading sits against a published boundary, and nothing more.

Frequently asked questions

What is AHA blood pressure classification?

The American Heart Association (AHA) classifies blood pressure into categories: Normal (less than 120/80), Elevated (120-129/less than 80), High Blood Pressure Stage 1 (130-139/80-89), High Blood Pressure Stage 2 (140+/90+), and Hypertensive Crisis (180+/120+).

Which number matters more, systolic or diastolic?

Your category is set by whichever number falls into the higher range. If your systolic and diastolic fall in different categories, the more severe one applies.

Can this checker replace my doctor's advice?

No. This is an informational reference using AHA cut-offs, not a medical diagnosis. Always consult your doctor for decisions about blood pressure management.
