## 0. Where we stand today

The site has **one** commercial page: [/folio/diary/](../folio/diary/index.html), pointing at `amazon.com/dp/B0H89T4VLP` — a US-only ASIN. Every other page is free content or a free tool. There is no shop, no cart, no email list, no `/subscribe/`, no price anywhere else on 44 pages.

That is not a weakness to be ashamed of; it is a clean slate. But it means we have no merchandising surface at all, and every competitor below has one.

## Part 2 — Competitor teardown

Five archetypes. For each: what they sell, what it costs, **how the site actually looks**, what to steal, what to avoid.

### 2.1 Intelligent Change — *the premium paper-first brand*
`intelligentchange.com` · The Five Minute Journal

**What they sell:** Journals · Productivity tools · Mindful Affirmations (card decks) · Conversation card games · Stationery · Lifestyle · Kids · Gift cards · **Bundles as a first-class collection**. Bundle example: journal + reminder cards, $57.60 (from $64).

**How it looks:** Full-bleed lifestyle photography, warm neutral palette, enormous social proof stacked above the fold — "Join 3+ million people", "Every 88 seconds someone chooses…", a "FEATURED ON" press-logo strip, and a continuously scrolling wall of verified customer reviews *with customer-uploaded photos*. Sustainability icons (100% compostable / plastic-free / recycled FSC paper) as a trust row. A **6-month money-back guarantee** repeated three times.

**Steal:**
- Bundles as their own navigation item — it raises AOV without a new product.
- Customer photos beside reviews. Far more persuasive than star counts.
- A named guarantee. Ours could be "print it, try a month, tell us if it didn't help."
- They have an **Apps** page — a paper company that added software. We are the mirror image and should say so.

**Avoid:** Their price point and photography budget. This is a funded consumer brand.

### 2.2 Passion Planner — *the volume planner brand, and the closest thing to a blueprint*
`passionplanner.com` · Shopify

**What they sell, in INR, to an Indian browser:**

| Product | Price |
|---|---|
| Digital Mindful Minute Journal | **Rs. 1,200** |
| Digital Self-Care Journal | **Rs. 800** |
| Weekly 2026–27 paper planner | Rs. 3,400 |
| Monthly Tabs | Rs. 500 |
| Routines & Chores sticker pack | Rs. 1,100 |
| Growth & Goals sticker book | Rs. 2,800 |

Proof: 3M+ planners sold, 1M+ users, 4.7 stars across 17,500+ reviews.

**How it looks:** Countdown-timer sale banner pinned to the top. Product grid with hover photography. A problem→solution block ("Don't stay stuck with: Overwhelm / Feeling Lost / Procrastination" → "Passion Planner helps: Define your goals / Break goals down / Take action today"). Testimonials from named athletes. A four-step "How It Works" (Define → Plan → Act → Reflect). Community/charity block. `#PASHFAM` Instagram wall.

**Steal — three things, and these are the most valuable findings in this document:**

1. **"Free Downloads" is a whole product collection**, sitting in the main shop navigation next to paid products, with its own copy: *"We believe everyone should have access to the tools and resources they need… free printable planner pages, guides, and worksheets."* This is precisely the free-sheet-as-funnel model, validated at scale by a company with 3M sales. It confirms our free printable should be **expanded and promoted**, not gated.
2. **Digital planners are a real paid category at Rs. 800–1,200 in India.** We had not considered this at all. It is a PDF with hyperlinked tabs for GoodNotes/Notability — no printing, no shipping, no print cost, and it does not compete with "free printable" search intent because the buyer is someone who already owns a tablet and a stylus and is shopping for a *designed* product, not a utility sheet.
3. **Give $15, Get $15 referral** and **bulk/group orders** as standing programmes.

**Avoid:** The countdown-timer urgency theatre. It would read as manipulative against our "no guilt, no streaks" positioning.

### 2.3 Baronfig — *Clear Habit Journal, the single-hero-product play*
`baronfig.com/products/clear-habit-journal` · $34

**Why this one matters most:** its interior is almost exactly the Folio Diary. The product gallery shows: a split-grid page, **habit tracker templates**, an index page, and a **one-line-per-day journal page**. That is Folio's paper concept, already shipped, by a design brand, in partnership with James Clear (*Atomic Habits*).

**What they sell:** The journal $34 · journal + Atomic Habits book bundle $27-ish uplift · journal + pen set **$85** · pens from $75 · plus Wholesale and a "For Business" customisation programme.

**How it looks:** Stark, product-first, near-white. No lifestyle clutter. The gallery is the entire argument — closed book, then interior spread after interior spread, so you understand the product before reading a word. "Designed in New York City / Made in USA". A 15% off first-order email capture modal.

**Steal:**
- **Show the interior pages.** Our `/folio/diary/` page has "A page from inside" — good — but the gallery should carry more spreads. This is what converts a paper product.
- The author/expert collaboration model. Our equivalent is a doctor's endorsement on a BP log.
- **Wholesale and For Business.** Same insight as E4 above, from a completely different category.

**Avoid:** Competing on manufacturing quality. They make physical objects; we print on demand.

### 2.4 Amazon.in KDP commodity sellers — *the crowd we would join*
Search `blood pressure log book` → **2,000+ results**

**What they look like:** Titles that are keyword soup — *"BLOOD PRESSURE LOGBOOK : Daily Personal Record and Monitor Tracking Numbers Blood Pressure Chart Sheets Details Blood pressure log for your daily measurements : Daily Personal Record and Monitor Tracking Sheets | Keep Track of Your Blood Pressure & Heart Health."* Covers are template stock art. Interiors are a table repeated 110 times. Almost all English, almost all authored abroad, sizes cluster at 6×9" and 5.8×8.5".

Notable exception proving the appetite for combination products: *ME IN 210 DAYS — A Combo Health Logbook* recording BP, sugar, pulse, temperature and oxygen together.

**What they are missing — our entire opening:** no Hindi, no bilingual, no large print for seniors, no doctor-visit summary page, no companion app, no brand, no aftercare.

**Avoid:** Keyword-soup titles. They rank, but they also signal "generated". A clean, confident title plus a differentiated subtitle is a better long game and matches our brand.

### 2.5 Indian stationery brands — *the category to stay out of*
Search `habit tracker journal` on Amazon.in → 654 results

Papboo, DOODLE, LAURET BLANC, myPAPERCLIP, AccuPrints, Zaslan, Kelenate, Trueflair, ORBY, EKDALI, and **Amazon's own brand Eono**. Hardcovers, 100–130 GSM paper specs quoted in titles, spiral binding, free stickers, fabric bags, bookmarks, gift boxes. The results page is saturated with Sponsored Products — several brands are bidding on their own category.

**Verdict:** we would be a print-on-demand paperback with no stickers and no ad budget, against brands whose listings mention GSM. **Do not enter.**

## Part 3 — What the teardown changes

1. **All three successful brands are paper-first and added apps later.** We are the inverse: the apps exist, are free, and already have users. That is a genuinely differentiated story — *"the app is free forever; the paper is for people who prefer paper"* — and none of them can tell it.

2. **Indians pay Rs. 800–3,400 for planner products.** Passion Planner charges it in INR on the Indian storefront. Our earlier "India won't pay" instinct was too broad. The correction: India won't pay for a *generic utility printable* (the "free" search intent is real) but does pay for a *designed, branded product*.

3. **Digital planners for tablets are the paid-digital category we missed.** Rs. 800–1,200, zero marginal cost, zero shipping, no collision with free-printable intent. For Folio this is close to free work — the diary interior already exists as a parametric HTML generator; a hyperlinked tablet edition is a re-export, not a redesign.

4. **The free printable should get bigger and louder, not gated.** Validated by the market leader running it as a shop collection.

5. **B2B keeps appearing from two unrelated directions** — Baronfig's "For Business", Passion Planner's "Group & Bulk". Nobody in the BP log crowd does it. Clinics and diagnostic labs handing a branded log book to hypertensive patients is a real, unserved channel.

## Part 4 — Ranked shortlist

Ordered by (value × certainty) ÷ effort.

| Rank | Do this | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **`/subscribe/` + expanded free printable collection** | Costs a day. Every competitor's funnel starts here. Ours does not exist |
| 2 | **List the Folio Diary on Amazon.in** | The product is already built and live on `.com` only — invisible to our own market. Pure upside |
| 3 | **BP monitor affiliate on the two BP how-to posts** | Reader is mid-purchase. Highest yield per hour |
| 4 | **Digital tablet edition of the Folio Diary** — Rs. 799 | Re-export of an existing generator. Rs. 800–1,200 is a proven India price |
| 5 | **BP log book on KDP** — bilingual, large print, doctor-visit page | Real shopping intent, crowded but undifferentiated, proven pipeline |
| 6 | **Shop page + bundles** | Merchandising surface. Bundles lift AOV with no new product |
| 7 | **Crumbs pricing page on the site** | Already-earning product with no proper storefront |
| 8 | **B2B outreach to clinics** with the BP log book | Unserved channel, but needs #5 to exist first |
| 9 | Sticker packs, posters, sleep-sound packs | Later, once a shop exists |
| — | Habit/planner journals · display ads · selling the harness | **Do not** |

## Open questions

1. **Hindi quality.** The bilingual differentiator only works if a human writes and checks the medical wording. Who?
2. **GST.** Currently individual KYC. KDP royalties and affiliate income both need a CA conversation before Rank 3 or 5 goes live.
3. **Where does the shop live?** A static `/shop/` page linking out to Amazon and a Merchant-of-Record platform costs nothing and needs no backend. A real cart does. Recommend the former until something proves it needs the latter.
4. **Author name on KDP** — PurposeLab Studio or a person? Affects the existing Folio Diary listing too.
